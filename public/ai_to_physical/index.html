02 · Project Lineage & Technical Progression

From Classic Anglepoise CAD to Workshop-Fabricable Product

KUMIKO-TRUSS did not emerge in isolation. It grew directly out of an earlier CAD engineering study reconstructing a classic spring-counterbalanced Anglepoise desk lamp in FreeCAD & Fusion — proving agents could model complex mechanical linkages digitally before pushing all the way into studio timber fabrication.

Precedent · Hand Sketch → Classic Anglepoise in CAD

Phase A: From Studio Sketch to Parametric Mechanical CAD

Starting from a single hand-drawn marker sketch, agents constructed a complete 57-part parametric FreeCAD B-Rep assembly (20 VarSet parameters, 11/11 automated clearance passes, 5.26 kg cast-iron base, spring-parallelogram kinematics, exported cleanly to .STEP and Fusion 360).

Key takeaway: Agents could already translate a studio sketch into valid, articulated metal CAD — raising the bigger question: what happens when we ask them to design for real machines we can actually run in our own workshop?

Evolution · KUMIKO-TRUSS (V6.2)

Phase B: Closing the Loop into Physical Fabrication

Shifted from spun metal & cast iron to accessible workshop processes — 6 mm laser-cut Baltic birch, 12 mm 1-sided CNC milling, and desktop Bambu X1C 3D printing — expanding to 79 parametric B-Rep parts with automated A2 nesting and headless multi-plate slicing.

Result: Moved from virtual CAD validation into a standing, articulated, illuminated physical artifact ready for high-finish timber production.

04 · AI Designing for the Physical World

Standout Mechanical Solutions Authored Entirely by AI

Beyond drafting and nesting, the agents independently engineered clever, manufacturing-aware physical mechanisms — demonstrating genuine mechanical reasoning about polymer compliance, print layer orientation, joinery topology, and thermal clearance.

AI Mechanism 01 · Highlight 100% Support-Free Print

4-Finger Compliant PETG Bulb Retention System (FDM_01C)

Conceived directly from a single photo of the user's IKEA LED1938R3 (GUZ5.3, 24V DC) puck. Four axially slotted compliant spring fingers feature inward double-cone retention beads that flex and snap cleanly into the bulb's circumferential mid-body groove — paired with a 45° self-supporting internal stop ledge, four pointed-arch convection/wire windows, and a +24 mm drop-down neck that cleared the rear socket without recutting a single piece of wood.

Ø45.6 mm slip-fit in Ø46.0 mm ring 40 mm socket headroom 3 underside snap-lock bumps
AI Mechanism 02 Laser Topology

Split Assemblable 50/50 Flush Half-Lap Kumiko Shade

Solved a subtle assembly deadlock automatically: an unbroken wrist arch prevents solid Crown and Rim rings from threading onto the shade. The agent split the structural spine into an interlocking SPINE_FRONT_WRIST and SPINE_BACK pinned with a Ø4 × 8 mm dowel, and added R0.7 mm micro-dogbones to every radial notch so all 16 ribs seat 100% flush.

16 flush curved ribs R0.7 mm laser micro-dogbones Unbroken structural rings
AI Mechanism 03 DFM & Ergonomics

Captive-Shoulder Scalloped Nut Knobs & Horizontal Crossbars

FDM_07 12-flute thumbwheels place a solid 4 mm plastic shoulder on the washer side so tightening standard M8/M6/M5 hex nuts pulls them deeper into the knob rather than popping them out. FDM_09 stepped crossbars include a subtle 0.55 mm bottom flat chord so they print horizontally with lengthwise layer lines for maximum shear strength.

Pull-in captive hex pocket Lengthwise FDM grain Auto-parallel 6 mm / 21 mm shoulders
AI Mechanism 04 1-Sided CNC Base

Single-Sided CNC Base with Integral 60° Yaw-Stop Lug

Engineered all three 12 mm base tiers to mill from one side only: leaving an uncut 60° birch lug inside Tier 2's turret counterbore for a crisp ±110° mechanical hard stop, locally counterboring a 3.0 mm web so the M7 dimmer nut gets full thread bite, trapping the rear DC bezel in a stepped T-slot, and keeping all 4 TPU feet outside the removable steel lid.

1.06 kg mild steel ballast ±110° wire-safe yaw stop Serviceable without peeling feet

Rapid Physical-Digital Iteration Log — Turning Trial-Build Observations into Production Files in Minutes
Iteration Trigger Workshop Observation Hands-Off Agentic Turnaround Outcome (V6.1 → V6.2)
#01
Late-stage specific bulb change (IKEA LED1938R3)		 Switched from generic E27 to an on-hand 24V IKEA GUZ5.3 puck whose tall rear socket met SPINE_FRONT_WRIST. Engineered FDM_01C (+24 mm drop-down compliant 4-finger snap holder) & sliced via Bambu CLI in minutes. 40 mm socket clearance verified on the lit build with zero wood recutting.
#02
Crown ring carrier slip-fit tuning		 Laser-cut Crown Ring (Ø46.0 mm) fit ribs perfectly; early thermal carrier barrel needed slip-fit clearance & old draft STL cleanup. Adjusted barrel OD to Ø45.6 mm with unbroken vent rings and archived legacy draft STL. Clean snap-in seating on both FDM_01 and FDM_01C holders.
#03
Adding 6 mm anti-deflection arm spacers		 Trial tightening showed subtle inward bow across the unsupported 6.0 mm inner cheek gap at P0 & E0. Inserted SPACER_P0/E0_6MM (Ø24 × Ø8.4 × 6 mm) into both A2 Illustrator laser nests (0 overlaps) & FDM_06 STL. Dead-parallel arms under full friction clamp — cuttable or printable.
#04
Upgrading off-the-shelf hardware to unified 3D prints		 In-house 3D printing proved effortless — designer opted to replace wing nuts & wood dowels with matching scalloped knobs and crossbars. Authored FDM_07 (12-flute nut knobs), FDM_08 (hollow turret pivot), and FDM_09 (horizontal stepped crossbars) + multi-plate Bambu slices. Signature cohesive orange hardware language & faster bench assembly.
#05
Consolidating FDM_08 into 1 solid part		 Inspecting the test print spotted that gluing a separate spigot ring onto the hollow pivot was an unnecessary carryover. Fused spigot, thrust collar, and barrel into a single stronger solid + re-sliced (30.5 min X1C, 1 tiny hidden support ring) & synced manual. Eliminated glue seam at peak cantilever bending stress; bearing face prints smooth face-up.
#06
Base CNC cycle time insight		 Using PS foam as a rapid Tier 1 proxy validated assembly fast while highlighting CNC milling time on thick pockets. Froze V6.2 CNC base for the Birch Hero while spawning Stream 3 (V7 KUMIKO-OPEN, 100% laser + 3D print). Direct evidence feeding the next-generation zero-CNC open-source fork.

07 · Design Education Provocation & Program Forks

Pedagogical Implications & Next Directions

Preliminary framing for the DEC 26 5-minute lightning talk and April 2027 End-of-Year Expo, alongside the two decoupled R&D forks launching from this frozen V6.2 baseline.

Pedagogy · Theme 01

Compressing Friction, Multiplying Iteration

Offloading parametric CAD scripting, A2 laser nesting, ISO drafting, and multi-plate Bambu slicing collapses prototype turnaround from days to minutes — unlocking far more real physical test-build cycles inside a studio semester.

Pedagogy · Theme 02

Technical Skill vs. Taste & Discernment

When AI can invent working physical mechanisms (like the 4-finger compliant bulb holder) and operate fabrication software hands-off, how should educators cultivate the material intuition and taste needed to direct and critique those outputs?

Pedagogy · Theme 03

The Shifting Role of the Graduate Designer

Graduate value moves rapidly from rote CAD-syntax operator toward Design Director, Rapid Prototype Auditor, and Physical-Digital Mediator — shifting curricula toward materiality, system architecture, and critical judgement.

Fork 03 · KUMIKO-OPEN (V7)

Fastener-Free Open-Source Build Kit

100% laser cutting + 3D printing — zero CNC milling and zero purchased nuts, bolts, screws, or washers.

  • Stacked 6 mm laser-cut base with sealed local-ballast chamber (sand/pebbles/coins)
  • Printed coarse-thread thumbbolts & compliant PETG friction clutches
  • Standardized twist-lock bayonet wrist for modular community shades

Fork 04 · KUMIKO-KINETIC (V8)

Twisting-Louver Shade & Local WiFi ESP32 Control

Two-stage kinetic & mechatronic exploration building on the modular arm architecture.

  • Stage 4A: Twisting crown lays all 16 slats flat/shingled to cut back & side glare
  • Stage 4B: ESP32 local-network web app driving motorized articulation + tunable color/dimming
  • Mechanical crown-twist rig validated manually on the bench before motorizing
